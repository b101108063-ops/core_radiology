  Three column concept
                  •   The three column concept of functional spinal anatomy helps to evaluate the stability
                      of thoracolumbar spinal trauma. A spinal injury where the integrity of two of the three
                      columns is preserved is generally considered stable, while a severe injury to all three
                      columns is most likely to be unstable. Two column injuries may be stable or unstable.
                  •   The anterior column is comprised of the anterior two thirds of the vertebral body,
                      including the anterior longitudinal ligament.
                  •   The middle column consists of the posterior third of the vertebral body and the
                      posterior longitudinal ligament.
                  •   The posterior column represents the posterior elements and the posterior ligaments
                      (consisting of the supraspinous and infraspinous ligaments, and the ligamentum flavum).
  Chance fracture
                  •   A Chance fracture, also known as a seat belt fracture, is a flexion distraction injury
                      caused by acute forward flexion of the spine during sudden deceleration.
                  •   There is horizontal splitting of the vertebra, beginning posteriorly in the spinous
                      process or lamina and extending anteriorly through the vertebral body.
  Spondylolysis
                  •   Spondylolysis is a fracture of the pars interarticularis, which may be secondary to
                      chronic stress or an acute fracture.
                  •   On oblique radiographs, spondylolysis is seen as a defect in the neck of the “scotty dog.”
                  •   Spondylolysis leads to spondylolisthesis, which is anterior–posterior subluxation of
                      one vertebral body relative to another.
