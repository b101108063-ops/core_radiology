Hypothenar hammer: Digital subtraction angiogram of the hand shows an approximately 2–3 cm
occlusion of the ulnar artery (between the blue arrowheads), and multifocal occlusions of the proper
palmar digital arteries in the 2nd through 4th digits (red arrowheads).

Digital subtraction angiogram of the wrist better shows the segmental occlusion of the ulnar artery
(between the blue arrowheads). At surgery, an ulnar artery aneurysm (not visible on angiography) was
found and treated.

Hypothenar hammer syndrome represents injury to the ulnar artery as it crosses the
hamate bone. Chronic repetitive trauma causes the ulnar artery to be chronically
traumatized at the hamate, leading to intimal injury, thrombus, aneurysm, or
pseudoaneurysm.
The classic clinical history of hypothenar hammer syndrome is a jackhammer operator
with ischemia of the fourth and fifth digits.
