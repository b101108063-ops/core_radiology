```
Hemodialysis access complications

- Thrombosis is the most common complication of AVFs and AVGs. It is usually due to underlying stenosis from neointimal hyperplasia.
- Stenosis typically occurs at the venous anastomosis (in AVGs) or at the fistula outflow vein (in AVFs).
- Treatment of thrombosed dialysis access includes surgical thrombectomy, percutaneous thrombolysis, or percutaneous mechanical thrombectomy.
- Angioplasty of the underlying stenosis is performed after thrombectomy to maintain patency.
- Infection is the second most common complication, especially in AVGs. Infected AVGs usually require complete excision and debridement.
- Pseudoaneurysm formation can occur at the puncture site, especially with repeated punctures in the same location.
- High-output cardiac failure can occur with large-bore fistulas, especially in elderly patients with limited cardiac reserve. Physical exam findings include bounding pulses and a widened pulse pressure. Treatment includes surgical fistula reduction or banding.

Dialysis access steal syndrome

- Dialysis access steal syndrome occurs when the fistula diverts blood away from the distal limb, causing ischemia.
- It is more common with brachial artery-based fistulas and in patients with peripheral vascular disease.
- Clinical presentation ranges from mild coolness and paresthesias to rest pain and tissue loss.
- Mild cases can be observed. Moderate to severe cases require fistula modification (banding, revision, or ligation).
```
