Upper extremity intervention
Sympathectomy

- Chemical sympathectomy involves injection of a neurolytic agent (e.g., alcohol or phenol) into the sympathetic chain to treat hyperhidrosis (excessive sweating), Raynaud phenomenon, or complex regional pain syndrome.
- Thoracic sympathectomy is performed for upper extremity hyperhidrosis or Raynaud.
- Lumbar sympathectomy is performed for lower extremity hyperhidrosis or Raynaud.
- Chemical sympathectomy can be performed percutaneously under fluoroscopic or CT guidance.
- Surgical sympathectomy involves thoracoscopic or laparoscopic division of the sympathetic chain.

Ulnar artery intervention

- Ulnar artery thrombosis (hypothenar hammer syndrome) occurs in patients who repetitively trauma the ulnar artery at the hook of hamate.
- Presentation includes hand pain, cold intolerance, and digital ischemia.
- Treatment includes medical management (antiplatelet therapy, smoking cessation), endovascular treatment (thrombolysis, angioplasty), or surgical excision with vein graft interposition.

Vascular malformations
Arteriovenous malformations (AVMs)

- AVMs are congenital vascular malformations consisting of a nidus of abnormal arteries and veins without an intervening capillary bed.
- AVMs can occur anywhere in the body but are most common in the brain, lungs, liver, and extremities.
- Clinical presentation depends on location and size. Symptoms include pain, swelling, bleeding, and high-output cardiac failure (with large AVMs).
- Embolization is the primary treatment. The goal is to occlude the nidus while preserving normal vessels.
- Surgery is reserved for small accessible lesions or when embolization is not feasible.
- Recurrence is common after both embolization and surgery.

Venous malformations (VMs)

- Venous malformations are low-flow malformations composed of dilated venous channels.
- They are the most common type of vascular malformation.
- VMs are typically present at birth and grow proportionally with the child.
- Symptoms depend on location and size. Superficial VMs present as bluish compressible masses. Deep VMs can cause pain, swelling, or functional impairment.
- Compression therapy is first-line for symptomatic VMs.
- Sclerotherapy is the primary interventional treatment. Common sclerosing agents include ethanol, polidocanol, and sodium tetradecyl sulfate (STS).
- Surgery is reserved for small accessible lesions or when sclerotherapy fails.
