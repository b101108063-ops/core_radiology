```
Thoracic outlet syndrome (TOS)

- Thoracic outlet syndrome (TOS) refers to compression of the neurovascular bundle at the thoracic outlet (the space between the clavicle and first rib).
- Neurogenic TOS (most common, 95%) presents with neurological symptoms including pain, paresthesias, and weakness in the arm and hand.
- Venous TOS (Paget-Schroetter syndrome) presents with arm swelling and effort-related venous thrombosis.
- Arterial TOS presents with arm claudication or digital ischemia.
- Imaging for TOS includes chest radiograph, cervical spine radiograph, MR/CT angiography in different arm positions, and conventional angiography.
- Treatment depends on the type and severity. Conservative management with physical therapy is first-line for neurogenic TOS. Venous TOS is treated with thrombolysis and first rib resection. Arterial TOS requires surgical decompression.

Arm and hand ischemia

- Acute arm ischemia is usually due to arterial embolism (often from atrial fibrillation) or arterial thrombosis.
- Chronic arm ischemia is most commonly due to atherosclerosis. Other causes include thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger disease), which typically affects young male smokers.
- The hand is the most common site of acute ischemia. Blue finger syndrome refers to acute digital artery occlusion.
- Raynaud phenomenon refers to episodic digital ischemia triggered by cold or emotional stress, caused by vasospasm of digital arteries. Primary Raynaud (Raynaud disease) is more common and benign. Secondary Raynaud (Raynaud phenomenon) is associated with connective tissue diseases.
- Treatment of Raynaud includes cold avoidance, smoking cessation, calcium channel blockers, and in severe cases, digital sympathectomy.
```
