```
Lymphatic interventions
Lymphangiography

- Lymphangiography is the imaging of lymphatic vessels after injection of contrast.
- Indications include evaluation of chylous disorders (chylothorax, chylous ascites), lymphatic leaks, and lymphatic malformations.
- The foot web spaces are injected with a lymphatic dye (e.g., isosulfan blue or methylene blue) to identify lymphatic vessels.
- A 25-30 gauge needle is inserted into an identified lymphatic vessel and Lipiodol is infused at 0.1-0.2 mL/min.
- Imaging is performed to opacify the lymphatics and assess for leaks or malformations.

Thoracic duct embolization

- Thoracic duct embolization is performed for chylothorax refractory to conservative management (dietary modifications, octreotide).
- The thoracic duct is cannulated from a peripheral lymphatic or from an intranodal approach.
- Embolization is performed with coils and/or NBCA glue.
- Technical success rates are high, but clinical success (resolution of chylothorax) is lower due to alternate chyle drainage pathways.

Treatment of lymphatic malformations

- Lymphatic malformations (lymphangiomas) are congenital low-flow malformations composed of dilated lymphatic channels.
- They are most common in the head and neck (cervical hygromas) but can occur anywhere.
- Treatment options include observation, sclerotherapy, surgery, or a combination.
- Sclerosing agents for lymphatic malformations include OK-432 (Picibanil), ethanol, bleomycin, and doxycycline.
```
