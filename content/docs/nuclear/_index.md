# Nuclear Imaging

![Page 562](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-562.png)

## PET-CT — Technical Considerations

![Page 563](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-563.png)
![Page 564](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-564.png)

### Basic PET Physics
- **PET radiotracer**: A positron-emitting radiotracer releases a positron that travels a short distance within tissue, then annihilates with an electron to produce two **511 keV photons** traveling approximately 180° apart. These coincident photons are detected by a circular crystal array.
- **F-18 FDG**: Fluorine-18 is a positron emitter with a 110-minute half-life. FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) is a glucose analog taken up by cells via **GLUT 1 and 3 transporters**, then phosphorylated by hexokinase and trapped intracellularly (cannot undergo glycolysis).

### SUV Quantification
- **Standardized Uptake Value (SUV)** = (ROI activity × body weight) / administered activity.
- SUV varies with equipment, time post-injection, glucose/insulin levels, etc. **Never diagnose malignancy solely by SUV.**
- Preferred method: Use background ratio (liver, cerebellum, or basal ganglia). Relative SUV: **<20% = mild**, **20–60% = moderate**, **>60% = intense** uptake.

### CT Correlation
- Modern PET-CT combines metabolic PET with anatomic CT.
- CT is used for **attenuation correction** and anatomic localization.
- Dense oral contrast or metal prostheses may cause attenuation artifacts (miscalculated FDG uptake).

### Patient Preparation
- **NPO ≥4 hours** before FDG injection; keep insulin at basal level.
- Blood glucose should be **<200 mg/dL** (preferably <150 mg/dL).
- After injection: rest quietly for **60 minutes**. Talking → vocal cord uptake; walking → muscle uptake.

### Normal FDG Distribution
- **Brain**: Intense uptake (brain relies on glucose).
- **Kidneys/ureters/bladder**: Very intense urine activity.
- **Salivary glands, tonsils, thyroid**: Mild to moderate symmetric uptake.
- **Liver**: Moderate, inhomogeneous uptake.
- **Bowel**: Diffuse mild-moderate uptake. **Metformin** can increase colonic uptake.
- **Heart**: Variable; prefers fatty acids but uses glucose if available.
- **Muscles**: Low normally; elevated insulin or recent exercise increases uptake.
- **Brown fat**: Supraclavicular and intercostal; mild-moderate uptake, especially if cold.

## PET-CT — Oncologic Indications

![Page 565](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-565.png)
![Page 566](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-566.png)
![Page 567](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-567.png)

### Lung Cancer
- **PET-CT** is used for initial staging and treatment response evaluation of **non-small cell lung cancer** (small-cell is treated as metastatic at diagnosis).
- Detects ~10% of patients with CT-negative distant metastases.
- **Very sensitive** for malignant lymph nodes; not specific. PET-positive mediastinal nodes require **mediastinoscopy** before denying curative surgery.

### Solitary Pulmonary Nodule (SPN)
- PET evaluates nodules **≥8 mm**.
- Most malignant SPNs are **FDG-avid**; low-grade tumors (bronchioloalveolar carcinoma, carcinoid) may be **falsely negative**.
- Active granulomatous disease (TB) may cause **false-positive** uptake.
- SUV alone cannot definitively diagnose benign or malignant nodules.

### Colon Cancer
- Limited role in local staging (poor spatial resolution, physiologic bowel uptake).
- **Primary role**: Evaluating **metastatic disease** — especially isolated hepatic metastases eligible for resection or ablation.
- Follow-up PET after treatment: delay **~2 months** due to flare phenomenon.

### Head and Neck Cancer
- Used for initial staging of **squamous cell carcinoma**, especially regional nodal metastases.
- Post-treatment specificity is limited by altered anatomy and inflammation; delay scans **≥4 months** after chemoradiation.

### Thyroid Cancer
- **FDG PET** is indicated when thyroid cancer is **radioiodine-resistant** (undifferentiated or medullary types) with rising thyroglobulin but negative I-131 whole-body scan.

### Lymphoma
- **Key role** in staging and restaging; most Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphomas are FDG-avid.
- Diffuse marrow uptake may be from **G-CSF stimulation**, chemotherapy rebound, or malignant infiltration. **Focal** uptake more likely indicates lymphoma involvement.

### Breast Cancer
- Used for recurrent or **stage IV** breast cancer.
- Not routinely indicated for stage I–III.

### Esophageal Cancer
- Identifies **stage IV** disease (non-surgical candidates).
- **≥30% decrease in SUV** after neoadjuvant therapy suggests favorable prognosis; lack of SUV decrease may spare ineffective chemotherapy.

### Cancers with Limited PET-CT Role
- **HCC**: Only ~50% are FDG-avid (high phosphatase dephosphorylates FDG).
- **Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)** and **bladder cancer**: ~50% FDG-avid; detection limited by high urine background.
- **Prostate cancer**: FDG PET not useful; **C-11 choline PET** is FDA-approved but not yet widespread.

## Nuclear Cardiology

![Page 568](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-568.png)

### Perfusion Imaging Overview

![Page 569](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-569.png)

- **Myocardial perfusion imaging** evaluates blood flow to the myocardium.
- Five key questions for any perfusion abnormality:
  1. **Reversible or fixed?** (ischemia vs. scar/hibernating myocardium)
  2. **Size?** (small / medium / large)
  3. **Severity?** (mild = subendocardial, moderate, severe = transmural)
  4. **Location?** (which coronary artery territory)
  5. **Associated findings?** (RV uptake, ischemic dilation, wall motion abnormality)

### Stress Methods
- **Physical**: Treadmill (Bruce or modified Bruce protocol). Target heart rate = **85% of maximal** (220 − age in years).
- **Pharmacologic-vasodilatory**:
  - **Dipyridamole**: adenosine deaminase inhibitor; caffeine/theophylline reverse effect; antidote: aminophylline 100–200 mg IV.
  - **Adenosine**: identical to dipyridamole; half-life ~30 sec; no reversal agent needed.
  - **Regadenoson**: adenosine receptor agonist; 2–3 min half-life; convenient single universal-dose IV.
- **Pharmacologic-adrenergic**: **Dobutamine** (β1 agonist); used when adenosine is contraindicated (severe asthma, COPD, recent caffeine).

### Clinical Applications

![Page 570](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-570.png)

- **Acute chest pain**: Negative perfusion scan allows safe discharge. Normal study = **0.6% annual cardiac event rate**, even in high-risk patients.
- **Coronary stenosis hemodynamic significance**: Normal nuclear perfusion = low event risk despite anatomic stenosis.
- **Post-MI risk stratification** — High-risk findings:
  - Significant peri-infarct ischemia
  - Defect in a different vascular territory (multi-vessel disease)
  - Significant lung uptake (LV dysfunction)
  - Left ventricular aneurysm
  - **EF <40%** on gated SPECT
- **Preoperative noncardiac surgery assessment**.
- **Viability assessment** prior to revascularization.
- **Post-CABG evaluation**.

### Viability Imaging

![Page 571](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-571.png)

- **Hibernating myocardium**: Hypoperfused but viable myocardium that may benefit from revascularization.
- **F-18 FDG PET** is the **gold standard** for myocardial viability (correlate with perfusion study):
  - **Mismatch** (perfusion defect + FDG uptake) = viable → revascularization may help.
  - **Match** (perfusion defect + no FDG uptake) = scar → medical therapy only.
- Gated SPECT wall motion in perfusion defect area: normal motion → viability; large defect with abnormal motion → scar.

### Radionuclides in Nuclear Cardiology

![Page 571](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-571.png)

| Agent | Modality | Half-life | Key Features |
|-------|----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Thallium-201** | SPECT | 73 hr | K⁺ analog; redistribution enables viability assessment; low energy → attenuation artifact |
| **Tc-99m sestamibi** | SPECT | 6 hr | No redistribution; fixed in myocardium; uptake proportional to perfusion |
| **Rubidium-82** | PET | 76 sec | K⁺ analog; generator-produced; pharmacologic stress only (exercise impractical) |
| **N-13 ammonia** | PET | 10 min | Cyclotron-produced; excellent resolution; preferred for quantitative MBF |
| **F-18 FDG** | PET | 110 min | **Viability agent only**; not a perfusion tracer |

### Imaging Protocols

![Page 572](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-572.png)
![Page 573](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-573.png)
![Page 574](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-574.png)

#### Single-Day Tc-99m Sestamibi Protocol
- Rest images first (8–10 mCi), then stress images (20–30 mCi at peak exercise or pharmacologic stress).
- Image **~30 min post-injection** to allow liver clearance (no redistribution with sestamibi).
- Gated SPECT: wall motion at time of imaging; perfusion at time of injection.

#### PET Perfusion
- Greater sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy vs. SPECT.
- Attenuation-correction CT improves accuracy by eliminating attenuation artifact.
- N-13 ammonia preferred over Rb-82 for **quantitative myocardial blood flow** (higher extraction fraction).

### Image Interpretation

![Page 575](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-575.png)

#### Systematic Approach
1. **Quality control**: Stress images should have higher signal-to-noise than rest (3× more tracer). If not → consider dose infiltration.
2. **Coronal/rotating MIP**: Check for motion artifact, breast attenuation, RV uptake (right heart disease/pulmonary hypertension), pulmonary uptake (LV dysfunction).
3. **Gated SPECT**: Evaluate wall motion and wall thickening.
4. **Perfusion defect analysis**:
   - Reversible = ischemia; fixed = scar or hibernating myocardium.
   - Severity: mild / moderate / severe.
   - Size: small (1–2 segments), medium (3–4), large (≥5).
   - Territory: **LAD**, **LCx**, or **RCA**.
5. **Transient ischemic dilation (TID)**: May indicate three-vessel disease even without focal defect.

### Reconstruction Axes and Myocardial Segments

![Page 576](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-576.png)

#### Three Reconstruction Axes
- **SA (Short Axis)**: "Donut" view from apex through base.
- **VLA (Vertical Long Axis)**: U-shaped view pointing left.
- **HLA (Horizontal Long Axis)**: U-shaped view pointing down.

#### 17-Segment Left Ventricular Model
| Level | Segments | Coronary Territory |
|-------|----------|-------------------|
| Basal | 6 (anterior, anterolateral, inferolateral, inferior, inferoseptal, anteroseptal) | LAD / LCx / RCA |
| Mid | 6 | LAD / LCx / RCA |
| Apical | 4 | LAD / LCx / RCA |
| Apex | 1 | LAD |

#### Sample Nuclear Cardiology Cases

![Page 576](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-576.png)

**Normal Study**: Rest and stress images are identical and normal.

**Fixed Defect**: Moderate-sized fixed defect in **mid and basal inferior wall (RCA territory)** — represents scar or hibernating myocardium.

**Reversible Defect**: Large partially reversible defect of severe intensity involving **anterior wall (LAD territory)** and **lateral wall (LCx territory)**.

## Thyroid Imaging

![Page 577](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-577.png)

### Radiotracers

![Page 577](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-577.png)

| Agent | Half-life | Energy | Administration | Primary Use |
|-------|-----------|--------|----------------|-------------|
| **I-131** | 8 days | 364 keV γ + β | Oral | **Therapy only** (thyroid cancer, Graves disease) |
| **I-123** | 13 hr | 159 keV γ | Oral | **Diagnostic imaging**; excellent detail + uptake quantitation |
| **Tc-99m pertechnetate** | 6 hr | 140 keV γ | IV | Diagnostic; not trapped; rapid washout; not quantified |

- **Tc-99m pertechnetate preferred** when: recent IV iodinated contrast administered, IV medication required, or rapid study needed.
- **I-123 preferred** when detailed imaging and quantitative uptake values are needed.

### Patient Preparation
- Non-suppressed **TSH** required: withhold thyroid hormone **4 weeks**, or administer **2 IM injections of recombinant TSH (rTSH)**.

### Pregnancy and Breast Feeding
- **I-131**: **Contraindicated in pregnancy** (crosses placenta; fetal thyroid iodine uptake begins at 12 weeks).
- **Breast feeding**: Permanently stop for I-131; resume 2–3 days post I-123; resume 12–24 hr post Tc-99m.

### Diagnostic Indications

![Page 578](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-578.png)

#### Ectopic Thyroid
- **I-123 or Tc-99m** localize ectopic thyroid tissue.
- **Lingual thyroid**: Base of tongue. **Struma ovarii**: Ovarian teratoma with functional thyroid tissue. **Substernal thyroid**: Usually substernal goiter.

#### Thyroid Nodule
- Imaged when cytology is indeterminate.
- **Hot nodule** (hyperfunctioning): Almost always benign adenoma.
- **Cold nodule**: ~20% malignancy risk (most common cause: benign colloid cyst ~70–75%).
- **Warm nodule**: Cold nodule with overlapping thyroid tissue → requires further investigation (biopsy if oblique views indeterminate).
- **Discordant nodule** (hot on Tc-99m, cold on I-123): Maintains technetium uptake but cannot trap iodine → **biopsy recommended** (may be malignant).

#### Graves Disease
- **Autoimmune hyperthyroidism** with thyromegaly, diffusely increased uptake, and often a prominent pyramidal lobe.
- Elevated 6-hr and 24-hr iodine uptake. Normal: 6-hr **6–18%**; 24-hr **10–30%**.
- Intense thyroid uptake may obscure salivary glands on both I-123 and Tc-99m.
- Treatment: **I-131 radiotherapy** (definitive), surgery, or antithyroid drugs (methimazole, PTU).

#### Hashimoto Thyroiditis
- Most common inflammatory thyroid disease; usually **hypothyroid**.
- Variable scan appearance: diffuse increased uptake (Graves-like) to patchy uptake (multinodular pattern).
- Patchiness reflects infiltration by **lymphocytes and lymphoid follicles**.

#### Subacute Thyroiditis
- Painful swollen gland or silent hyperthyroidism.
- **Low radiotracer uptake** with very low 24-hr uptake (~4.9%).
- Self-limited condition; treat symptomatically (NSAIDs or steroids in severe cases).

### Therapeutic Indications (I-131)

![Page 579](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-579.png)
![Page 580](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-580.png)

#### Thyroid Carcinoma (Post-Thyroidectomy)
- I-131 administered **~1–2 months** post-thyroidectomy to treat and image residual/metastatic disease.
- Withhold thyroid replacement → TSH rises to **30–50 µIU/mL** to maximize I-131 uptake.
- **Low-risk** (<1.5 cm, no capsule invasion): **<30 mCi** I-131.
- **High-risk**: **100–200 mCi** I-131.
- Emerging approach: **30 mCi standard dose** for T1, T2, N1 cancers.

#### Post-Radioiodine Monitoring
- Follow **thyroglobulin** levels. Rising thyroglobulin → I-123 scan for recurrence.
- **Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies** preclude thyroglobulin monitoring.

#### Treatment of Graves Disease
- Single oral dose of I-131. >90% efficacy.
- Contraindications: pregnancy, lactation, inability to comply with radiation safety.
- Fixed dose up to **15 mCi** is equally effective as calculated dose.

#### Toxic Multinodular Goiter
- I-131 reduces goiter size; may need higher dose than Graves disease; multiple treatments possible.
- Typical dose: **25 mCi**.

## Parathyroid

![Page 580](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-580.png)

### Tc-99m Sestamibi — Dual-Phase Imaging
- Uptaken by both parathyroid and thyroid; thyroid activity washes out faster than parathyroid.
- **Early images**: Both thyroid and parathyroid adenoma show increased uptake.
- **Delayed images**: Thyroid washes out; **parathyroid adenoma retains** tracer (persistent focal activity).
- Thyroid adenoma also washes out on delayed images (distinguishes from parathyroid).
- If indeterminate: **Tc-99m pertechnetate** can be added (parathyroid tissue does not localize pertechnetate).

## Gastrointestinal Imaging

![Page 581](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-581.png)

### Liver–Spleen Imaging (Tc-99m Sulfur Colloid)
- Sulfur colloid taken up by **reticuloendothelial cells** (Kupffer cells in liver, splenic macrophages, bone marrow).
- **80–90%** goes to liver; remainder to spleen and bone marrow. Biologic half-life: **2–3 minutes**.

#### Focal Decreased Hepatic Uptake (Photopenic Defect)
- Most common cause: **hepatic cyst**.
- Most hepatic masses cause decreased uptake: **HCC, adenoma, abscess**.
- In a patient with cirrhosis or chronic hepatitis risk factors, focal decreased uptake raises concern for **HCC**.

#### Focal Increased Hepatic Uptake
- **Focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH)**: Usually normal or mildly increased (contains Kupffer cells). ~1/3 appear photopenic.
- Regenerating nodule in cirrhosis: focal increased uptake.
- **Budd-Chiari syndrome** (hepatic vein thrombosis): Increased caudate lobe uptake (late stage).

#### Colloid Shift
- Increased splenic and bone marrow uptake → suggests **liver dysfunction** (most commonly cirrhosis).

#### Diffuse Pulmonary Uptake
- Nonspecific; seen in cirrhosis, COPD with infection, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, high serum aluminum.

#### Focal Nodular Hyperplasia (FNH)
- FNH contains Kupffer cells → usually normal or increased sulfur colloid uptake.
- Hepatic adenoma lacks Kupffer cells → consistently cold defect.
- **HIDA scan** as adjunct: FNH contains biliary ductules → positive on HIDA.

#### Intrapancreatic Spleen
- Confirmed by sulfur colloid scan (shows uptake); Tc-99m tagged RBC study also an option.

### Gastrointestinal Bleeding (Tc-99m Tagged RBCs)

![Page 582](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-582.png)
![Page 583](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-583.png)

- **In vitro labeling**: 1–3 mL anticoagulated blood + stannous chloride + Tc-99m; ~95% labeling efficiency; procedure >20 min.
- Detects bleeding rates as low as **0.2 mL/min** (angiography requires 1 mL/min).
- Positive finding: activity **changing shape and position over time** due to peristalsis → pattern suggests small vs. large bowel source.
- Alternative: **Tc-99m sulfur colloid** (rapid blood clearance; vascular half-life 2–3 min).

### Meckel Imaging (Tc-99m Pertechnetate)
- Pertechnetate localizes to **gastric mucosa**.
- **Meckel diverticulum**: Embryologic omphalomesenteric duct remnant; **10–60% contain ectopic gastric mucosa** → mucosal damage and GI bleeding.
- Positive scan: focal increased activity in **right lower quadrant**.
- Lateral view confirms anterior location (not ureter).
- Other RLQ causes: appendicitis, intussusception (diffuse hyperemia pattern).

### Hepatobiliary Imaging (HIDA)

![Page 584](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-584.png)
![Page 585](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-585.png)

#### Radiotracers: Tc-99m IDA Agents
- **Disofenin**: visualizes biliary system with bilirubin up to **20 mg/dL**; 90% hepatic uptake.
- **Mebrofenin**: up to **30 mg/dL**; 98% hepatic uptake.
- Both actively transported into hepatocytes; not conjugated.

#### HIDA Protocol
- **NPO 6 hours**, but patient must have eaten within 24 hours.
- If NPO >24 hr: give **CCK** (0.02 µg/kg slow infusion) to empty gallbladder first; wait ≥2 hours after CCK before exam.
- Dynamic imaging begins immediately after injection.
- **Gallbladder visualized by 15 min** → acute cholecystitis ruled out.
- Gallbladder not seen by 1 hr: give **morphine** (0.04 mg/kg, max 4 mg) → contracts sphincter of Oddi, redirects bile to cystic duct.
- Morphine only if tracer seen in small bowel (patent CBD). If morphine contraindicated: extend imaging to 4 hours total.

#### Normal HIDA
- Liver visible by 5 min; gallbladder by 15 min; small bowel by 30–60 min (patent CBD confirmed).

#### Acute Cholecystitis
- **Nonvisualization of gallbladder** after 90 min + morphine = **86–98% sensitive** for acute cholecystitis.
- **Rim sign**: increased hepatic activity around gallbladder fossa → hyperemia, possibly gangrenous cholecystitis.
- False positives: recent meal, prolonged fasting, CCK given, pancreatitis, severe illness, chronic cholecystitis, TPN.
- False negatives: acalculous cholecystitis with patent cystic duct, duodenal diverticulum.

#### Chronic Cholecystitis
- Long-standing inflammation → loss of normal gallbladder function.
- Most have **normal HIDA scan** (no single pathognomonic pattern).
- **Gallbladder ejection fraction (GBEF) <35%** after CCK suggests chronic cholecystitis (reliability controversial).

#### Biliary Leak
- HIDA accurately detects biliary leaks.
- Post-cholecystectomy: tracer pooling in gallbladder fossa or right paracolic gutter (cystic duct stump leak).
- Right lateral decubitus position promotes dependent pooling.

#### Severe Hepatic Dysfunction
- Poor hepatic uptake, delayed blood pool clearance.
- Hepatic mass (e.g., cholangiocarcinoma) → focal **photopenic defect** (functioning hepatocytes are needed to extract tracer).

## Pulmonary Imaging

![Page 586](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-586.png)

### Radiotracers

#### Tc-99m-MAA (Perfusion)
- **Macro-aggregated albumin (MAA)** lodges in pulmonary capillary bed (particles 10–30 µm).
- Standard dose: **3–5 mCi** (200,000–600,000 particles); particle fragments break down in ~30 min.
- **Half-dose** (~100,000 particles) in children, pregnancy, mild pulmonary hypertension, right-to-left shunt.
- **Severe pulmonary hypertension**: relative contraindication (capillary obstruction risk).
- **Right-to-left shunt**: immediate renal and brain uptake after IV injection (MAA bypasses lungs).
- Free pertechnetate also causes brain/renal uptake but is differentiated by **thyroid uptake** (present in free Tc-99m, absent in R-L shunt).

#### Xenon-133 (Ventilation)
- Inhaled gas; half-life 5.3 days; 81 keV γ + β; critical organ is trachea.
- **Washin-washout imaging** detects air trapping (COPD).
- Imaged posteriorly to avoid breast attenuation.
- Requires patient cooperation (closed spirometer), negative pressure room, exhaust system.

#### Tc-99m DTPA (Ventilation)
- Technetium-labeled aerosol; particles remain in lungs 20–60 min; 30 mCi typical.
- Easier than Xenon: multiple projections, no exhaust needed, portable.

### V/Q Scanning in Pulmonary Embolism

![Page 587](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-587.png)
![Page 588](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-588.png)

#### PE in Pregnancy
- **Leading cause of death** in pregnancy in developed countries.
- **CT pulmonary angiography**: maternal breast dose **10–70 mGy** (>>ACR mammography 3 mGy/breast); fetal dose 0.1–0.66 mGy.
- **Perfusion scanning**: maternal breast dose **<0.31 mGy**; fetal dose 0.1–0.37 mGy.
- In pregnant patients with **normal chest radiograph**: nuclear perfusion scanning is recommended.
- Technique: usually Q-only (perfusion only); half standard MAA dose (~100,000 particles).
- Diagnostic accuracy equivalent between PE-CT and Q-only perfusion scanning.
- CT advantage: alternative diagnosis not seen on plain film (~5% of cases), at cost of 100× higher breast radiation.
- **PIOPED II criteria**: High probability V/Q scan = **97% specificity**, **88% PPV** for pulmonary embolism.

## Bone and Musculoskeletal Imaging

![Page 589](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-589.png)
![Page 590](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-590.png)

### Tc-99m MDP Bone Scintigraphy
- **Tc-99m MDP (methylene diphosphonate)** — incorporated into hydroxyapatite crystal at sites of active bone formation.

#### Three-Phase Bone Scan
1. **Perfusion phase** (immediate): Vascular flow.
2. **Blood pool phase** (3–5 min): Soft tissue.
3. **Delayed phase** (2–4 hr): Skeletal uptake.

#### Common Indications
- **Metastatic disease**: Most common malignant bone tumor in adults; osteolytic (breast, lung, kidney, thyroid) or osteoblastic (prostate, carcinoid) pattern.
- **Osteomyelitis**: Increased uptake on all three phases; periosteal reaction on delayed.
- **Stress fracture**: Linear cortical uptake; differentiates from osteomyelitis by pattern.
- **Avascular necrosis (AVN)**: "Cold" area surrounded by increased uptake (repair rim).
- **Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)**: Diffusely increased periarticular uptake.
- **Paget disease**: Massively increased uptake in affected bone (skull, spine, pelvis, femur).

## Kidneys

![Page 591](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-591.png)
![Page 592](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-592.png)
![Page 593](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-593.png)

### Radiotracers
- **Tc-99m DMSA**: Static cortical imaging; evaluates cortical defects (scars, cysts, tumors).
- **Tc-99m MAG3**: Dynamic renography; tubular secretion; evaluates function and drainage.
- **Tc-99m DTPA**: Glomerular filtration; used for GFR estimation.

### Clinical Applications
- **Renocystogram (VCUG)**: Detect **vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)**.
- **Renal artery stenosis**: MAG3 with captopril challenge — decreased uptake post-captopril indicates hemodynamically significant stenosis.
- **Acute pyelonephritis**: Focal or diffuse decreased cortical uptake on DMSA.
- **Renal scar**: DMSA cortical defects matching old VUR sites.
- **Split renal function**: MAG3 or DTPA clearance ratios.

### Brain Perfusion SPECT
- **Tc-99m HMPAO** or **Tc-99m ECD** — lipophilic agents that cross the blood-brain barrier and convert to hydrophilic form intracellularly.
- Clinical applications:
  - **Stroke**: Hypoperfusion patterns; luxury perfusion in acute stroke.
  - **Dementia**: Alzheimer's (bitemporal/parietal hypoperfusion), frontotemporal, Lewy body.
  - **Seizure**: Ictal hyperperfusion; interictal hypoperfusion.
  - **Brain death**: Absent cortical uptake with preserved cerebellar perfusion (**halo sign**).

### Cerebral Circulation and Metabolism
- **Carotid artery stenosis**: Quantitative CBF measurements pre- and post-carotid endarterectomy.
- **Crossed cerebellar diaschisis**: Cerebellar hypometabolism secondary to supratentorial lesion.
- **FDG PET brain**:
  - **Alzheimer disease**: Hypometabolism in posterior cingulate and temporoparietal regions.
  - **Epilepsy**: Interictal hypometabolic focus.
  - **Brain tumors**: Grading correlates with FDG uptake (high-grade tumors more FDG-avid).

### Whole-Body Imaging: Infection and Inflammation
- **FDG PET-CT** is highly sensitive for detecting infection and inflammation.
- **Indications**: Fever of unknown origin (FUO), prosthetic joint infection, vasculitis, sarcoidosis.
- Mechanism: Activated inflammatory cells (neutrophils, macrophages) have high glucose metabolism → intense FDG uptake.
- **Limitations**: Not specific for infection vs. inflammation; high physiological bowel uptake can mask pathology.

### Nuclear Medicine in Pediatrics
- **Radiation exposure** is a greater concern in children (higher tissue sensitivity, longer life expectancy).
- Dose reduction strategies: half-dose MAA in perfusion scanning, weight-based dosing.
- **Sedation** may be required for long imaging protocols.
- Common pediatric applications:
  - **VCUG (renocystogram)**: VUR detection.
  - **DMSA renal cortical scan**: Pyelonephritis, renal scarring.
  - **Meckel scan**: Ectopic gastric mucosa detection.
  - **Bone scan**: Metastatic osteosarcoma, Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease, slipped capital femoral epiphysis.
  - **MIBG (I-123/I-131)**: Neuroblastoma staging and follow-up.

### Quality and Safety in Nuclear Medicine
- **Time, distance, shielding**: Fundamental principles of radiation protection.
- **Decay schemes**: Understanding particle vs. photon emission guides safety precautions (I-131 requires special handling due to β emission).
- **Image quality**: Adequate count statistics are essential; attenuation correction improves accuracy; motion correction (respiratory/cardiac gating) improves lesion detection.
- **Pregnancy screening**: Female patients of childbearing potential should be screened before administering radionuclides.
- **Patient release**: Patients administered certain radionuclides (e.g., I-131 for thyroid cancer) may require hospitalization or specific instructions for release.
