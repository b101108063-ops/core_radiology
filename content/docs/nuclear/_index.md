## Technical Considerations (pages 563–564) {#technical-considerations-pages-563564}

![Page 554](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-563.png)
![Page 555](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-564.png)

### Basic PET Physics {#basic-pet-physics}
- **PET radiotracer**: A positron-emitting radiotracer releases a positron that travels a short distance within tissue, then annihilates with an electron to produce two 511 keV photons traveling ~180° apart.
- These coincident photons are detected by a circular crystal array.
- **F-18 FDG**: Fluorine-18 is a positron emitter with a 110-minute half-life. FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) is a glucose analog taken up by cells via **GLUT 1 and 3 transporters**, then phosphorylated by hexokinase and trapped intracellularly.

### SUV Quantification {#suv-quantification}
- **Standardized Uptake Value (SUV)** = (ROI activity × body weight) / administered activity.
- SUV varies with equipment, time post-injection, glucose/insulin levels, etc. **Never diagnose malignancy solely by SUV.**
- Preferred: Use background ratio (liver, cerebellum, or basal ganglia). Relative SUV: <20% = mild, 20–60% = moderate, >60% = intense uptake.

### CT Correlation {#ct-correlation}
- Modern PET-CT combines metabolic PET with anatomic CT.
- CT is used for **attenuation correction** and localization.
- Dense oral contrast or metal prostheses may cause attenuation artifacts.

### Patient Preparation {#patient-preparation}
- **NPO ≥4 hours** before FDG injection; keep insulin at basal level.
- Blood glucose should be <200 mg/dL (preferably <150 mg/dL).
- After injection: rest quietly for 60 minutes. Talking → vocal cord uptake; walking → muscle uptake.

### Normal FDG Distribution {#normal-fdg-distribution}
- **Brain**: Intense uptake (brain relies on glucose).
- **Kidneys/ureters/bladder**: Very intense urine activity.
- **Salivary glands, tonsils, thyroid**: Mild to moderate symmetric uptake.
- **Liver**: Moderate, inhomogeneous uptake.
- **Bowel**: Diffuse mild-moderate uptake. Metformin can increase colonic uptake.
- **Heart**: Variable; prefers fatty acids but uses glucose if available.
- **Muscles**: Low normally; elevated insulin or recent exercise increases uptake.
- **Brown fat**: Supraclavicular and intercostal; mild-moderate uptake, especially if cold.

## Oncologic Indications (pages 565–567) {#oncologic-indications-pages-565567}

![Page 556](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-565.png)
![Page 557](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-566.png)
![Page 558](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-567.png)

### Lung Cancer {#lung-cancer}
- **PET-CT** is used for initial staging and treatment response evaluation of **non-small cell lung cancer**.
- Detects ~10% of patients with CT-negative metastases.
- Sensitive for malignant lymph nodes; not specific. PET-positive mediastinal nodes require **mediastinoscopy** before denying surgery.

### Solitary Pulmonary Nodule (SPN) {#solitary-pulmonary-nodule-spn}
- PET evaluates nodules ≥8 mm.
- Most malignant SPNs are **FDG-avid**; low-grade tumors (bronchioloalveolar carcinoma, carcinoid) may be falsely negative.
- Active granulomatous disease (TB) may cause **false-positive** uptake.
- SUV alone cannot definitively diagnose benign or malignant nodules.

### Colon Cancer {#colon-cancer}
- Limited role in local staging (poor spatial resolution, bowel uptake).
- **Primary role**: Evaluating **metastatic disease** — especially for isolated hepatic metastases that can be resected.
- Follow-up PET: delay ~2 months post-treatment due to flare phenomenon.

### Head and Neck Cancer {#head-and-neck-cancer}
- Used for initial staging of **squamous cell carcinoma**, especially nodal metastases.
- Post-treatment specificity is limited due to inflammation; delay scans **≥4 months** after chemoradiation.

### Thyroid Cancer {#thyroid-cancer}
- **Differentiated thyroid carcinoma**: FDG PET is useful for suspected radioiodine-resistant recurrence when **thyroglobulin is rising but the radioiodine scan is negative**.
- **Undifferentiated and medullary thyroid carcinoma** may be FDG-avid despite absent radioiodine uptake. Medullary carcinoma arises from parafollicular C cells and is followed with **calcitonin and CEA**, not thyroglobulin.

### Lymphoma {#lymphoma}
- **Key role** in staging and restaging; most Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphomas are FDG-avid.
- Diffuse marrow uptake may be from G-CSF, chemotherapy rebound, or malignant infiltration. **Focal** uptake more likely indicates lymphoma.

### Breast Cancer {#breast-cancer}
- Used for recurrent or **stage IV** breast cancer.
- Not routinely used for stage I–III.

### Esophageal Cancer {#esophageal-cancer}

![Page 559](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-568.png)

- Identifies **stage IV** disease (non-surgical candidates).
- ≥30% decrease in SUV after neoadjuvant therapy suggests favorable prognosis.

### Cancers with Limited PET-CT Role {#cancers-with-limited-pet-ct-role}
- **HCC**: Only ~50% are FDG-avid (high phosphatase dephosphorylates FDG).
- **Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)** and **bladder cancer**: ~50% FDG-avid; detection limited by high urine background.
- **Prostate cancer**: FDG PET not useful; C-11 choline PET is FDA-approved but not widespread.


## Nuclear Cardiology (pages 568–576) {#nuclear-cardiology-pages-568576}

### Clinical Overview — Perfusion Imaging {#clinical-overview--perfusion-imaging}

![Page 560](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-569.png)

#### Perfusion Imaging Overview {#perfusion-imaging-overview}
- **Myocardial perfusion imaging** evaluates blood flow to the myocardium.
- Five key questions for any perfusion abnormality:
  1. **Reversible or fixed?** (ischemia vs. scar)
  2. **Size?** (small/medium/large)
  3. **Severity?** (mild=subendocardial, moderate, severe=transmural)
  4. **Location?** (which coronary artery territory)
  5. **Associated findings?** (RV uptake, ischemic dilation, wall motion abnormality)

#### Stress Methods {#stress-methods}
- **Physical**: Treadmill (Bruce protocol). Target: 85% of maximal heart rate (220 − age).
- **Pharmacologic-adrenergic**: Dobutamine (β1 agonist; for asthma/COPD/caffeine users).
- **Pharmacologic-vasodilatory**: 
  - **Dipyridamole**: adenosine deaminase inhibitor; caffeine/theophylline reverse effect; antidote: aminophylline.
  - **Adenosine**: identical to dipyridamole, half-life ~30 sec, no reversal needed.
  - **Regadenoson**: adenosine receptor agonist, 2–3 min half-life, convenient single-dose IV.

### Clinical Applications {#clinical-applications}

- **Acute chest pain**: Negative perfusion scan → safe discharge. Normal study = **0.6% annual cardiac event rate**.
- **Coronary stenosis hemodynamic significance**: Normal nuclear perfusion = low event risk despite anatomic stenosis.
- **Post-MI risk stratification**: High-risk findings include peri-infarct ischemia, multi-vessel territory defect, significant lung uptake, LV aneurysm, EF <40%.
- **Preoperative noncardiac surgery assessment**.
- **Viability assessment** prior to revascularization.
- **Post-CABG evaluation**.

### Viability Imaging {#viability-imaging}

![Page 561](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-570.png)


- **Hibernating myocardium**: Hypoperfused but viable myocardium that may benefit from revascularization.
- **F-18 FDG PET** is the **gold standard** for viability imaging (correlate with perfusion study).
  - **Mismatch** (perfusion defect + FDG uptake) = viable → revascularization may help.
  - **Match** (perfusion defect + no FDG uptake) = scar → medical therapy only.
- Gated SPECT wall motion: normal motion in perfusion defect area → viability (hibernating myocardium); large defect with abnormal motion → scar.

### Radionuclides in Nuclear Cardiology {#radionuclides-in-nuclear-cardiology}

![Page 562](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-571.png)

### Imaging Protocols {#imaging-protocols}

![Page 563](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-572.png)
#### Single-Day Tc-99m Sestamibi Protocol {#single-day-tc-99m-sestamibi-protocol}
- Rest images first (8–10 mCi), then stress images (20–30 mCi at peak exercise or pharmacologic stress).
- Image ~30 min post-injection to allow liver clearance.
- Gated SPECT: wall motion at time of imaging; perfusion at time of injection.

#### PET Perfusion {#pet-perfusion}
- Greater sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy vs. SPECT.
- Attenuation-correction CT improves accuracy.
- N-13 ammonia preferred over Rb-82 for quantitative myocardial blood flow (higher extraction fraction).

### Image Interpretation {#image-interpretation}

#### Systematic Approach {#systematic-approach}
1. **Quality control**: Stress images should have higher signal-to-noise than rest (3× more tracer). Consider infiltration if not.
2. **Coronal/rotating MIP**: Motion artifact, breast attenuation, RV uptake (right heart disease/pulmonary hypertension), pulmonary uptake (LV dysfunction).
3. **Gated SPECT**: Wall motion and wall thickening.
4. **Perfusion defect analysis**:
   - Reversible = ischemia; fixed = scar or hibernating myocardium.
   - Severity: mild/moderate/severe.
   - Size: small (1–2 segments), medium (3–4), large (≥5).
   - Territory: LAD, LCx, or RCA.
5. **Transient ischemic dilation (TID)**: May indicate three-vessel disease even without focal defect.

### Reconstruction Axes and Myocardial Segments {#reconstruction-axes-and-myocardial-segments}

![Page 564](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-573.png)

#### Three Reconstruction Axes {#three-reconstruction-axes}
- **SA (Short Axis)**: "Donut" view from apex through base.
- **VLA (Vertical Long Axis)**: U-shaped view pointing left.
- **HLA (Horizontal Long Axis)**: U-shaped view pointing down.

#### 17-Segment Model {#17-segment-model}
- **Basal**: 6 segments (anterior, anterolateral, inferolateral, inferior, inferoseptal, anteroseptal).
- **Mid**: 6 segments.
- **Apical**: 4 segments.
- **Apex**: 1 segment.
- Color-coded by coronary territory: LAD (yellow), LCx (red), RCA (green).

## Thyroid Imaging (pages 577–580) {#thyroid-imaging-pages-577580}

![Page 566](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-575.png)

### Radiotracers {#radiotracers}


| Agent | Half-life | Energy | Administration | Primary Use |
|-------|-----------|--------|----------------|-------------|
| **I-131** | 8 days | 364 keV γ, β | Oral | Therapy only (thyroid cancer, Graves disease) |
| **I-123** | 13 hr | 159 keV γ | Oral | Diagnostic imaging; excellent detail; uptake quantitation |
| **Tc-99m pertechnetate** | 6 hr | 140 keV γ | IV | Diagnostic imaging; not trapped/washed out; not quantified |

- **Tc-99m pertechnetate preferred** when: recent IV iodinated contrast given, IV medication needed, or quick study required.
- **I-123 preferred** for detailed thyroid imaging and uptake values.

### Patient Preparation {#patient-preparation-1}
- **Differentiated-thyroid-cancer radioiodine imaging or therapy** requires TSH stimulation. This can be achieved by withholding exogenous thyroid hormone for about 4 weeks or by giving 2 IM injections of **recombinant TSH (rTSH)**.
- TSH stimulation is **not routine preparation** for evaluation of hyperthyroidism, thyroid nodules, or ectopic thyroid tissue; preparation should instead be tailored to the indication and recent iodine/medication exposure.

### Pregnancy and Breast Feeding {#pregnancy-and-breast-feeding}
- **I-131**: Contraindicated in pregnancy (crosses placenta; fetal iodine uptake begins at 12 weeks).
- **Breast feeding**: Must stop permanently for I-131; can resume 2–3 days post I-123; 12–24 hr post Tc-99m.

### Diagnostic Indications {#diagnostic-indications}

![Page 567](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-576.png)

#### Ectopic Thyroid {#ectopic-thyroid}
- **I-123 or Tc-99m** localize ectopic thyroid tissue.
- **Lingual thyroid**: Base of tongue. **Struma ovarii**: Ovarian teratoma with functional thyroid. **Substernal thyroid**: Usually substernal goiter.

#### Thyroid Nodule {#thyroid-nodule}
- Imaged when cytology indeterminate.
- **Hot nodule** (hyperfunctioning): Almost always benign adenoma.
- **Cold nodule**: ~20% malignancy risk (most commonly benign colloid cyst ~70–75%).
- **Warm nodule**: Cold nodule with overlapping thyroid tissue; requires further investigation.
- **Discordant nodule** (hot on Tc-99m, cold on I-123): Maintains technetium uptake but cannot trap iodine → biopsy recommended.

#### Graves Disease {#graves-disease}
- **Autoimmune hyperthyroidism** with thyromegaly, diffuse increased uptake, often a prominent pyramidal lobe.
- Elevated 6-hr and 24-hr iodine uptake. Normal: 6-hr 6–18%; 24-hr 10–30%.
- Thyroid uptake may be so intense that salivary glands are not visible.
- Treatment: **I-131 radiotherapy** (definitive), surgery, or antithyroid drugs (methimazole, PTU).

#### Hashimoto Thyroiditis {#hashimoto-thyroiditis}
- Most common inflammatory thyroid disease; usually **hypothyroid**.
- Variable scan appearance: diffuse increased uptake (Graves-like) to patchy uptake (multinodular goiter pattern).
- Patchiness from infiltration by lymphocytes and lymphoid follicles.

#### Subacute Thyroiditis {#subacute-thyroiditis}

![Page 568](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-577.png)

- Painful swollen gland or silent hyperthyroidism.
- **Low radiotracer uptake** and very low 24-hr uptake (~4.9%).
- Self-limited; treat symptomatically (NSAIDs or steroids).

### Therapeutic Indications (I-131) {#therapeutic-indications-i-131}

#### Thyroid Carcinoma (Post-Thyroidectomy) {#thyroid-carcinoma-post-thyroidectomy}
- I-131 given ~1–2 months post-thyroidectomy to treat/image residual/metastatic disease.
- Withhold thyroid replacement → TSH rises to 30–50 µIU/mL to maximize uptake.
- **Low-risk** (<1.5 cm, no capsule invasion): <30 mCi I-131.
- **High-risk**: 100–200 mCi I-131.
- New approach: 30 mCi standard dose for T1, T2, N1 cancers.

#### Post-Radioiodine Monitoring {#post-radioiodine-monitoring}
- Follow **thyroglobulin** levels. Rising thyroglobulin → I-123 scan for recurrence.
- Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies preclude thyroglobulin monitoring.

#### Treatment of Graves Disease {#treatment-of-graves-disease}
- Single oral dose of I-131. >90% efficacy.
- Contraindications: pregnancy, lactation, inability to comply with radiation safety.
- Fixed dose up to 15 mCi is equally effective as calculated dose.

#### Toxic Multinodular Goiter {#toxic-multinodular-goiter}
- I-131 reduces goiter size. May need higher dose than Graves disease; multiple treatments possible.
- Typical dose: 25 mCi.

## Parathyroid (page 580) {#parathyroid-page-580}

![Page 569](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-578.png)

### Radiotracer: Tc-99m Sestamibi {#radiotracer-tc-99m-sestamibi}
- Uptaken by both parathyroid and thyroid; thyroid activity decreases over time faster than parathyroid.

### Parathyroid Adenoma — Dual-Phase Imaging {#parathyroid-adenoma--dual-phase-imaging}
- **Early images**: Both thyroid and adenoma show increased uptake.
- **Delayed images**: Thyroid activity usually washes out faster, while a **parathyroid adenoma may retain** tracer as a persistent focus.
- Washout is variable: thyroid nodules may retain sestamibi and cause false-positive foci, while some parathyroid adenomas wash out. Correlate indeterminate findings with thyroid imaging and, when available, subtraction imaging or SPECT/CT.
- Tc-99m pertechnetate can be added for subtraction because parathyroid tissue does not take up pertechnetate.

## Gastrointestinal Imaging (pages 581–585) {#gastrointestinal-imaging-pages-581585}

![Page 570](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-579.png)

### Liver–Spleen Imaging (Tc-99m Sulfur Colloid) {#liverspleen-imaging-tc-99m-sulfur-colloid}
- Sulfur colloid taken up by **reticuloendothelial cells** (Kupffer cells in liver, splenic macrophages, bone marrow).
- 80–90% goes to liver; remainder to spleen and bone marrow.
- Biologic half-life: 2–3 minutes.

#### Focal Decreased Hepatic Uptake (Photopenic Defect) {#focal-decreased-hepatic-uptake-photopenic-defect}
- Most common cause: **hepatic cyst**.
- Most hepatic masses cause decreased uptake: HCC, adenoma, abscess.
- In a patient with cirrhosis or chronic hepatitis risk factors, focal decreased uptake raises concern for **HCC**.

#### Focal Increased Hepatic Uptake {#focal-increased-hepatic-uptake}
- **Focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH)**: Usually appears normal or mildly increased (contains Kupffer cells). ~1/3 appear photopenic.
- Regenerating nodule in cirrhosis: focal increased uptake.
- **Budd-Chiari syndrome**: Increased caudate lobe uptake (late stage).

#### Colloid Shift {#colloid-shift}
- Increased splenic and bone marrow uptake → suggests **liver dysfunction** (most commonly cirrhosis).

#### Diffuse Pulmonary Uptake {#diffuse-pulmonary-uptake}
- Nonspecific: cirrhosis, COPD with infection, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, high serum aluminum.

#### Focal Nodular Hyperplasia (FNH) {#focal-nodular-hyperplasia-fnh}
- FNH contains Kupffer cells → usually normal or increased sulfur colloid uptake.
- Hepatic adenoma lacks Kupffer cells → consistently cold defect.
- **HIDA scan** is an adjunct: FNH contains biliary ductules → positive on HIDA.

#### Intrapancreatic Spleen {#intrapancreatic-spleen}
- Confirmed by sulfur colloid scan (uptake confirms splenic tissue).

### Gastrointestinal Bleeding (Tc-99m Labeled RBCs) {#gastrointestinal-bleeding-tc-99m-labeled-rbcs}

![Page 571](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-580.png)
- **In vitro labeling**: 1–3 mL anticoagulated blood + stannous chloride + Tc-99m; ~95% labeling efficiency; takes >20 min.
- Detects bleeding rates as low as **0.2 mL/min** (angiography: 1 mL/min).
- Positive finding: activity changing shape/position over time due to peristalsis → pattern suggests small vs. large bowel source.
- Alternative: Tc-99m sulfur colloid (rapid blood clearance, 2–3 min vascular half-life).

### Meckel Imaging (Tc-99m Pertechnetate) {#meckel-imaging-tc-99m-pertechnetate}

- Pertechnetate localizes to **gastric mucosa**.
- **Meckel diverticulum**: Embryologic omphalomesenteric duct remnant; 10–60% contain ectopic gastric mucosa → bleeding.
- Positive scan: focal increased activity in **right lower quadrant**.
- Other RLQ causes of increased uptake: appendicitis, intussusception (more diffuse hyperemia).

### Hepatobiliary Imaging (HIDA) {#hepatobiliary-imaging-hida}

![Page 572](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-581.png)
#### Radiotracers: Tc-99m IDA Agents {#radiotracers-tc-99m-ida-agents}
- **Disofenin**: visualizes biliary system up to bilirubin 20 mg/dL; 90% hepatic uptake.
- **Mebrofenin**: up to bilirubin 30 mg/dL; 98% hepatic uptake.
- Both actively transported into hepatocytes; not conjugated.

#### HIDA Protocol {#hida-protocol}
- **NPO 6 hours**, but must have eaten within 24 hours. If NPO >24 hr: give CCK (0.02 µg/kg slow infusion) to empty gallbladder first.
- Wait ≥2 hours after CCK before starting exam.
- Dynamic imaging: gallbladder visualized by 15 min → acute cholecystitis ruled out.
- Gallbladder not seen by 1 hr: give **morphine** (0.04 mg/kg, max 4 mg) → contracts sphincter of Oddi, redirects bile to cystic duct.
- Morphine only if tracer seen in small bowel (patent CBD). If morphine contraindicated: image 4 hours total.

#### Normal HIDA {#normal-hida}

- Liver visible by 5 min; gallbladder by 15 min; small bowel by 30–60 min (patent CBD).

#### Acute Cholecystitis {#acute-cholecystitis}

![Page 573](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-582.png)

- **Nonvisualization of gallbladder** after 90 min + morphine = 86–98% sensitive for acute cholecystitis.
- **Rim sign**: increased hepatic activity around gallbladder fossa → hyperemia, possibly gangrenous cholecystitis.
- False positives: recent meal, prolonged fasting, CCK given, pancreatitis, severe illness, chronic cholecystitis, TPN.
- False negatives: acalculous cholecystitis with patent cystic duct, duodenal diverticulum.

#### Chronic Cholecystitis {#chronic-cholecystitis}

- Long-standing inflammation → loss of normal gallbladder function.
- Most have **normal HIDA scan** (no single pathognomonic pattern).
- **Gallbladder ejection fraction (GBEF) <35%** after CCK suggests chronic cholecystitis (controversial).

#### Biliary Leak {#biliary-leak}

![Page 574](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-583.png)

- HIDA accurately detects biliary leaks.
- Post-cholecystectomy patients: tracer pooling in gallbladder fossa or paracolic gutter.
- Right lateral decubitus position promotes dependent pooling.

#### Hepatic Dysfunction {#hepatic-dysfunction}

- Severe hepatic dysfunction → poor hepatic uptake, delayed blood pool clearance.
- Hepatic mass (e.g., cholangiocarcinoma) → focal **photopenic defect**.


## Pulmonary Imaging (pages 586–591) {#pulmonary-imaging-pages-586591}

![Page 575](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-584.png)

### Radiotracers {#radiotracers-1}

#### Tc-99m-MAA (Perfusion) {#tc-99m-maa-perfusion}
- **Macro-aggregated albumin (MAA)** lodges in pulmonary capillary bed (particles 10–30 µm).
- Standard dose: 3–5 mCi (200,000–600,000 particles).
- Half-dose (100,000 particles) in children, pregnancy, mild pulmonary hypertension, right-to-left shunt.
- **Severe pulmonary hypertension** is a relative contraindication (capillary obstruction risk).
- **Right-to-left shunt**: immediate renal and brain uptake after IV injection ( Tc-99m MAA bypasses lungs).
- Free pertechnetate also causes brain/renal uptake but is differentiated by thyroid uptake (present in free Tc-99m, absent in R-L shunt).
- MAA clumping: if drawn back into syringe and mixed with blood → coagulation artifacts.

#### Xenon-133 (Ventilation) {#xenon-133-ventilation}
- Inhaled gas; half-life 5.3 days; 81 keV γ + β emission.
- **Washin-washout imaging** detects air trapping (COPD).
- Imaged posteriorly to avoid breast attenuation.
- Requires patient cooperation (closed spirometer breathing), negative pressure room, exhaust system.

#### Tc-99m DTPA (Ventilation) {#tc-99m-dtpa-ventilation}
- Technetium-labeled aerosol; particles stay in lungs 20–60 min.
- 30 mCi typical dose.
- Easier than Xenon: multiple projections, no exhaust needed, portable.

### V/Q Scanning in Pulmonary Embolism {#vq-scanning-in-pulmonary-embolism}

![Page 576](/core_radiology/images/nuclear/page-585.png)
#### PE in Pregnancy {#pe-in-pregnancy}
- **Leading cause of death** in pregnancy in developed countries.
- **CT pulmonary angiography**: maternal breast dose 10–70 mGy (>>ACR mammography 3 mGy/breast); fetal dose 0.1–0.66 mGy.
- **Perfusion scanning**: maternal breast dose <0.31 mGy; fetal dose 0.1–0.37 mGy.
- In pregnant patients with **normal chest radiograph**: nuclear perfusion scanning is recommended.
- Technique: usually Q-only (perfusion only); half standard MAA dose (~100,000 particles).
- Diagnostic accuracy equivalent between PE-CT and Q scanning.
- CT advantage: alternative diagnosis not seen on plain film (~5% of cases), at cost of 100× breast radiation.
- **PIOPED II**: High probability V/Q scan = 97% specificity, 88% PPV for pulmonary embolism.

### Radiotracer and Protocol {#radiotracer-and-protocol}
- **Tc-99m MDP** is a technetium-labeled diphosphonate. A typical adult dose is 20 mCi, with delayed skeletal imaging at 2–4 hours.
- Rapid renal excretion is normal; diffuse soft-tissue uptake can occur in renal failure. The patient should void before imaging so bladder activity does not obscure a pelvic lesion.
- A **three-phase bone scan** includes: (1) radionuclide angiographic flow images every few seconds, where increased flow indicates hyperemia; (2) immediate blood-pool images of extracellular distribution; and (3) delayed skeletal images at about 3 hours.

### Metastatic and Extraskeletal Patterns {#metastatic-and-extraskeletal-patterns}
- Lower-probability patterns in a patient with known malignancy include a solitary rib focus (malignant only about 10% of the time), similar uptake in two adjacent ribs (usually trauma), and multiple adjacent photopenic lesions (consider infarction, avascular necrosis, or radiation change).
- Higher-probability patterns include a solitary sternal lesion in breast cancer (metastatic about 80% of the time), multifocal uptake in nonadjacent ribs, and a solitary photopenic lesion in a patient with malignancy—especially neuroblastoma, renal cell carcinoma, or thyroid cancer.
- Soft-tissue uptake may accompany recent brain or myocardial infarction, malignant pleural effusion or ascites, calcified soft-tissue metastases, recent breast procedures, breast carcinoma, myositis ossificans, dermatomyositis, or rhabdomyolysis.
- A **superscan** has diffusely intense osseous uptake with absent or very faint renal visualization. Metastatic prostate cancer is the commonest cause; breast cancer and lymphoma can also cause it. Metabolic superscans from primary or secondary hyperparathyroidism emphasize long bones, whereas metastatic superscans predominantly involve the axial skeleton and proximal humeri/femora. Renal failure can also obscure the kidneys but usually produces diffuse soft-tissue uptake.

### Primary Tumors and Fractures {#primary-tumors-and-fractures}
- **Osteosarcoma** usually shows markedly increased uptake, sometimes throughout the affected limb. **Ewing sarcoma** is intensely and homogeneously active and may be positive on all three phases.
- **Osteoid osteoma** has an intensely active vascular nidus surrounded by less intense hyperemia—the **double-density sign**. Differential considerations include osteoid osteoma, Brodie abscess, and less often stress fracture.
- Acute fractures show surrounding increased uptake; 95% are positive after 1 day in patients younger than 65, although skull fractures rarely show activity. Uptake becomes more focal at 2–3 months and gradually declines during healing; about 40% remain abnormal after 1 year.
- A toddler fracture is a spiral tibial fracture in a recently ambulatory child; bone scintigraphy is useful when radiographs in a limping child are negative.
- **Stress fractures** are usually positive by the time pain begins and are positive on all three phases. Common sites include the tibial diaphysis, femoral neck, and metatarsals (most often the second or third).
- **Shin splints** show normal flow and blood-pool phases with linear delayed uptake along the posteromedial tibia.
- **Insufficiency fractures** result from normal stress on abnormal osteoporotic bone; a sacral insufficiency fracture may show H-shaped uptake (the **Honda sign**).

### Prostheses, Infection, and Other Bone Disorders {#prostheses-infection-and-other-bone-disorders}
- Periprosthetic activity may be normal for up to 12 months after cemented arthroplasty and up to 2 years after noncemented arthroplasty. Later focal activity at the lesser trochanter and distal femoral stem tip suggests loosening; generalized periprosthetic uptake suggests osteomyelitis. Mild-to-moderate greater-trochanter/intertrochanteric activity often reflects heterotopic ossification.
- **Osteomyelitis** is positive on all three phases; this pattern is most specific when the radiograph is normal. With an underlying fracture or prosthesis, combine In-111 or Tc-99m labeled WBC imaging with Tc-99m sulfur-colloid marrow imaging: focal WBC uptake in an area without colloid uptake supports osteomyelitis. Gallium uptake exceeding bone-scan uptake can also increase specificity.
- **Cellulitis** increases flow and blood-pool activity but not delayed skeletal activity. **Septic arthritis** is positive on all three phases on both sides of the joint. **Discitis** increases skeletal activity in two adjacent vertebral bodies.
- **Hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy**, most often associated with lung cancer, produces parallel cortical lines of uptake along long-bone diaphyses.
- **Avascular necrosis** initially has decreased activity, followed by hyperemic increased uptake; SPECT may show a rim of increased uptake around central photopenia. Spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee commonly shows intense uptake in the medial femoral condyle.
- **Paget disease** has lytic, mixed, and sclerotic phases. The lytic phase is usually bone-scan positive despite possibly negative radiographs; the mixed phase is positive on both; scan activity may subside in the sclerotic phase despite persistent radiographic change. A new focal cold lesion raises concern for necrosis within malignant degeneration to osteosarcoma.
- **Complex regional pain syndrome** causes diffusely increased juxta-articular delayed uptake in multiple small joints of the hand or foot; flow and blood-pool activity vary but are most often increased.

### 腎元與鮑曼氏間隙（Bowman’s space） / Bowman's Space and ACE Inhibitor Renography {#腎元與鮑曼氏間隙bowmans-space--bowmans-space-and-ace-inhibitor-renography}

**鮑曼氏間隙（Bowman’s space）** 為腎小體（腎小球）外的囊狀空間，其外側緊接腎小球，內側接續近端小管（proximal tubule）；腎小球濾過率（GFR）即在此形成原尿。ACE inhibitor 腎圖檢查就是利用出球小動脈舒張使 GFR 下降的特性來判讀腎動脈狹窄。 Bowman’s space is the capsular space outside the glomerulus that continues into the proximal tubule, where glomerular filtrate is formed; ACE inhibitor renography exploits the efferent arteriolar relaxation that lowers the GFR.

**重點摘要**
- **檢查原理與檢查前準備**：給予 ACE inhibitor（通常是 captopril 25–50 mg）後，出球小動脈舒張、GFR 下降；已在使用 ACE inhibitor 的病人應於檢查前停藥 48 hours 至 1 week，並維持靜脈通路，以便低血壓時進行液體補充。 After administration of an ACE inhibitor (typically captopril, 25–50 mg), the efferent arterioles relax and the GFR decreases; if a patient is already on an ACE inhibitor, it should be stopped for 48 hours to 1 week prior to the exam, and intravenous access should be maintained in case fluid resuscitation is needed to treat hypotension.
- **陽性檢查的判讀重點**：檢查結果取決於放射藥物（MAG3 或 DTPA），但其特徵是 ACE inhibitor 後的腎圖變得異常或更加異常。 A positive study depends on the radiotracer (MAG3 or DTPA), but the hallmark is a post-ACE inhibitor renogram that becomes abnormal or more abnormal.
- **MAG3（腎小管型藥物）的陽性標準**：即使 GFR 下降，攝取與分泌大致保留，但 GFR 進一步下降會造成尿量減少與分泌藥物自收集系統清除減少；陽性標準包括任一腎在 2–3 minutes 的攝取 <40%、皮質活性相差 20%，或到達尖峰活性的時間比 ACE inhibitor 前延遲超過 2 minutes。 For MAG3 (a tubular agent), uptake and secretion are generally preserved even with a reduced GFR, but further decrease in GFR results in decreased urine production and decreased washout of secreted agent from the collecting system; positive criteria include <40% uptake by one kidney at 2–3 minutes, a difference in cortical activity of 20%, or a delay in time to peak activity of more than 2 minutes compared to the pre-ACE inhibitor study.
- **DTPA（腎小球型藥物）**：與 MAG3 不同，GFR 下降會造成 DTPA 的攝取與排泄都減少。 For DTPA (a glomerular agent), unlike MAG3, a decreased GFR causes diminished uptake and excretion of DTPA.
- **影像表現**：腎元示意圖標示鮑曼氏間隙（Bowman’s space）與近端小管（proximal tubule），並附 ACE inhibitor 前、後的 MAG3 與 DTPA 腎圖對照。 The nephron diagram labels Bowman’s space and the proximal tubule, and MAG3 and DTPA renograms are shown before and after ACE inhibitor administration.

#### 銦-111 噴曲肽掃描（Indium-111 pentetreotide, Octreoscan） / Indium-111 Pentetreotide (Octreoscan) {#銦-111-噴曲肽掃描indium-111-pentetreotide-octreoscan--indium-111-pentetreotide-octreoscan}

銦-111 噴曲肽掃描（Indium-111 pentetreotide, Octreoscan）主要用於類癌（carcinoid）造影。銦-111 由迴旋加速器製造，經電子捕獲衰變並放出 173 與 247 keV 的兩個光子，半衰期為 67 小時。噴曲肽是奧曲肽（octreotide）的類似物，可偵測具有生長抑素受體的腫瘤，包括胺前驅物攝取與脫羧腫瘤（amine precursor uptake and decarboxylation tumors, APUD tumors）。 An Indium-111 pentetreotide study (Octreoscan) is used primarily to image carcinoid. **重點摘要**

- **主要用途**：Octreoscan 主要用於類癌造影。Octreoscan is used primarily to image carcinoid.

#### 利尿劑腎圖（Diuretic renogram） / Diuretic Renography for Hydronephrosis {#利尿劑腎圖diuretic-renogram--diuretic-renography-for-hydronephrosis}

**利尿劑腎圖（diuretic renogram）** 在有腎積水（hydronephrosis）時，用來區分收集系統擴張是阻塞性或非阻塞性；檢查同時評估腎功能與尿流動力學，放射藥物以 Tc-99m MAG3 為主，較少使用 Tc-99m DTPA。 If hydronephrosis is present, a diuretic renogram can distinguish between obstruction and a nonobstructive cause of collecting system dilation; both renal function and urodynamics are evaluated, and the radiotracer is Tc-99m MAG3, much less commonly Tc-99m DTPA.

**重點摘要**
- **檢查目的與放射藥物**：利尿劑腎圖可區分阻塞與非阻塞性的收集系統擴張，同時評估腎功能與尿流動力學；放射藥物為 Tc-99m MAG3，較少用 Tc-99m DTPA。 A diuretic renogram can distinguish between obstruction and a nonobstructive cause of collecting system dilation, evaluating both renal function and urodynamics; the radiotracer is Tc-99m MAG3, much less commonly Tc-99m DTPA.
- **Furosemide 給藥**：約 20 minutes 的影像後給予 40 mg IV lasix（furosemide），腎功能不佳的病人可能需要較高劑量。 After approximately 20 minutes of imaging, 40 mg IV lasix is administered, and a higher dose of lasix may be needed in patients with renal insufficiency.
- **曲線判讀**：固定性機械阻塞時腎圖曲線在給藥後沒有變化；非阻塞性腎積水時，利尿後腎臟的額外壓力會撐開收集系統，讓放射性藥物由腎臟排出。 A fixed mechanical obstruction shows no change in the renogram curve after lasix, whereas in non-obstructive hydronephrosis the additional pressure of the diuresed kidney opens up the collecting system and allows drainage of radiotracer from the kidney.
- **清除半衰時間的閾值**：給藥後清除半衰時間 <10 minutes 為正常，10–20 minutes 為邊界值，>20 minutes 則提示阻塞。 After administration of lasix, a clearance half-time <10 minutes is normal, 10–20 minutes is borderline, and >20 minutes suggests obstruction.
- **偽陽性**：偽陽性（即在沒有真正阻塞時仍判為阻塞）可見於脫水、膀胱過度充盈或腎衰竭（對利尿劑的反應降低）。 False positives, meaning a diuretic renogram is positive for obstruction in the absence of a true obstruction, can be seen in dehydration, distended bladder, or renal failure with decreased response to diuretics.

#### 放射性核素膀胱造影（Radionuclide cystography, RNC） / Radionuclide Cystography for Pediatric Reflux {#放射性核素膀胱造影radionuclide-cystography-rnc--radionuclide-cystography-for-pediatric-reflux}

**放射性核素膀胱造影（radionuclide cystography, RNC）** 是評估小兒膀胱輸尿管逆流（reflux）最敏感的檢查，與排尿膀胱輸尿管攝影（voiding cystourethrogram）相比輻射暴露較少。使用的放射藥物不固定，Tc-99m pertechnetate、Tc-99m DTPA 與 Tc-99m sulfur colloid 效果都很好；不論選用哪一種，放射性核素都是經由導尿管逆行注入膀胱。 Radionuclide cystography is the most sensitive test for evaluation of pediatric reflux, with less radiation exposure compared to voiding cystourethrogram; Tc-99m pertechnetate, Tc-99m DTPA, and Tc-99m sulfur colloid all work well, and regardless of the radiotracer chosen, the radionuclide is injected retrograde into the catheterized bladder.

**重點摘要**
- **檢查敏感度**：RNC 是評估小兒逆流最敏感的檢查，且輻射暴露少於排尿膀胱輸尿管攝影。 Radionuclide cystography is the most sensitive test for evaluation of pediatric reflux, with less radiation exposure compared to voiding cystourethrogram.
- **放射藥物與注射方式**：Tc-99m pertechnetate、Tc-99m DTPA 與 Tc-99m sulfur colloid 皆可使用；不論選用哪一種，放射性核素均經導尿管逆行注入膀胱。 The radiotracer chosen is variable; Tc-99m pertechnetate, Tc-99m DTPA, and Tc-99m sulfur colloid work well, and the radionuclide is injected retrograde into the catheterized bladder regardless of the choice.
- **輕度逆流（RNC grade I）**：逆流侷限於輸尿管。 Minimal reflux (RNC grade I): reflux is confined to the ureter.
- **中度逆流（RNC grade II）**：逆流向上延伸至腎盂腎盞系統。 Moderate reflux (RNC grade II): reflux extends superiorly to the pelvicalyceal system.
- **重度逆流（RNC grade III）**：嚴重逆流造成迂曲的輸尿管及／或擴張的腎內系統。 Severe reflux (RNC grade III): severe reflux causing a tortuous ureter and/or dilated intrarenal system.

#### 銦-111 奧辛標記白血球（Indium-111 oxine leukocytes） / Indium-111 Oxine Leukocytes (WBCs) {#銦-111-奧辛標記白血球indium-111-oxine-leukocytes--indium-111-oxine-leukocytes-wbcs}

#### 鎵與鉈合併造影（Combined gallium and thallium imaging） / Combined Gallium and Thallium Imaging {#鎵與鉈合併造影combined-gallium-and-thallium-imaging--combined-gallium-and-thallium-imaging}

鎵與鉈合併造影（combined gallium and thallium imaging）可用於免疫功能低下患者，區分卡波西肉瘤（Kaposi sarcoma）、淋巴瘤（lymphoma）與結核病（tuberculosis）。卡波西肉瘤攝取鉈而不攝取鎵，結核病與非典型分枝桿菌攝取鎵而不攝取鉈，淋巴瘤則同時攝取鎵與鉈。鉈過去亦曾作為心肌造影劑。 Combined gallium and thallium imaging can be used in immunocompromised patients to distinguish Kaposi sarcoma, lymphoma, and tuberculosis. Kaposi sarcoma is thallium avid but does not take up gallium; tuberculosis and atypical mycobacteria take up gallium but not thallium; lymphoma takes up both. Thallium was also historically used as a myocardial agent.

**重點摘要**

- **鑑別用途**：鎵掃描結合鉈造影，可在免疫功能低下患者中區分卡波西肉瘤、淋巴瘤與結核病。Gallium scanning combined with thallium imaging can distinguish Kaposi sarcoma, lymphoma, and tuberculosis in immunocompromised patients.

### Kidneys {#kidneys}

#### Radiotracers {#radiotracers-2}
- **Tc-99m DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid)**: Static cortical imaging; evaluates renal cortical defects (scars, cysts, tumors).
- **Tc-99m MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine)**: Dynamic renography; tubular secretion; evaluates function and drainage.
- **Tc-99m DTPA**: Glomerular filtration; used for GFR estimation.

#### Clinical Applications {#clinical-applications-1}
- **Radionuclide cystography (RNC)**: Detects vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) with less radiation exposure than the fluoroscopic comparator, the voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG).
- **Renal artery stenosis**: MAG3 with captopril challenge — decreased uptake post-captopril indicates hemodynamically significant stenosis.
- **Acute pyelonephritis**: Focal or diffuse decreased cortical uptake on DMSA.
- **Renal scar**: DMSA defects matching old VUR sites.
- **Split renal function**: MAG3 or DTPA clearance ratios.

### Cerebrovascular {#cerebrovascular}

#### Brain Perfusion SPECT {#brain-perfusion-spect}
- **Tc-99m HMPAO** or **Tc-99m ECD** — lipophilic agents that cross BBB and convert to hydrophilic form intracellularly.
- Used for:
  - **Stroke**: Hypoperfusion patterns; luxury perfusion in acute stroke.
  - **Dementia**: Alzheimer's (bitemporal/parietal hypoperfusion), frontotemporal, Lewy body.
  - **Seizure**: Ictal hyperperfusion; interictal hypoperfusion.
  - **Brain death**: No intracranial perfusion or activity in either the cerebral hemispheres or the cerebellum. Planar flow imaging may show the nonspecific **hot-nose sign** from collateral flow.

#### Cerebral Circulation Studies {#cerebral-circulation-studies}
- **Carotid artery stenosis**: Quantitative CBF measurements pre- and post-carotid endarterectomy.
- **Crossed cerebellar diaschisis**: Cerebellar hypometabolism secondary to supratentorial lesion.

#### FDG PET Brain {#fdg-pet-brain}
- **Alzheimer disease**: Hypometabolism in posterior cingulate and temporoparietal regions.
- **Epilepsy**: Hypometabolic focus interictally.
- **Brain tumors**: Grading correlates with FDG uptake (high-grade tumors more FDG-avid).
