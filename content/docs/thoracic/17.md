The secondary pulmonary lobule (SPL) is the elemental unit of lung function.
Each SPL contains a central artery (the aptly named centrilobular artery) and a central
bronchus, each branching many times to ultimately produce acinar arteries and
respiratory bronchioles.
On CT, the centrilobular artery is often visible as a faint dot. The centrilobular bronchus is not
normally visible.
The acinus is the basic unit of gas exchange, containing several generations of branching
respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts, and alveoli.
There are generally 12 or fewer acini per secondary lobule.

•
•

Pulmonary veins and lymphatics collect in the periphery of each SPL.
Connective tissue, called interlobular septa, encases each SPL.
Thickening of the interlobular septa can be seen on CT and suggests pathologic enlargement of
either the venous or lymphatic spaces, as discussed on subsequent pages.

•

Each SPL is between 1 and 2.5 cm in diameter.
