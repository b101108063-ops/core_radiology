Like RB-ILD, desquamative interstitial pneumonia (DIP) is both the clinical syndrome
and the pathologic diagnosis. RB, RB-ILD, and DIP represent a continuous spectrum of
smoking-related lung disease.
Like RB, brown-pigmented macrophages are involved in DIP; however, sheets of these
abnormal macrophages also extend into the alveoli in DIP.

Imaging of DIP shows diffuse basal-predominant patchy or subpleural ground glass
opacification, more extensive than RB-ILD. Although the predominant abnormality is
ground glass, a few cysts may also be present.
